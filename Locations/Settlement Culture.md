Over two thousand years of relative isolation and calm from global upheavals, a human-monster subculture arose in the settlement, where a massive beastfolk individual is perceived as a being of a higher bodily and social order. For some people, closeness to him means fear, prestige, dependence, protection, sexual submission, and a higher position in the internal hierarchy.

This subculture has its own language of hints. Everyday speech may use words such as favorite, claimed, marked, temporary, closed, released, or under patronage. These are not necessarily official terms; more often they are gossip, family phrasing, and half-hints through which residents understand a young woman's position beside a beastfolk individual.

External signs also matter. Jewelry, bracelets, collars styled as social accessories, special clothing, scent marks, or claw marks may be perceived as elements of fashion, personal choice, or status. Inside the settlement, many understand their second meaning: a connection to a large hybrid, his attention, his patronage, or his power over a human.

A beastfolk individual may secretly choose a woman, draw her close, keep her near, demonstrate his rights to her before others, and even arrange her position, although in an age of high technology this looks more like a simulation, and the young woman can still break the bond with the hybrid. Inside the settlement, such a scene does not necessarily cause shock. Those around her primarily see it as an honor for the young woman to be under the patronage of an animal hybrid, as long as it does not threaten the lives of residents, the perimeter, the Herald, or external secrecy.

Settlement culture includes human favorites, concubines, dependent companions, and temporarily claimed women, though long-term women attached to beastfolk are rare. Families under the patronage of a large hybrid are also rare. Long-term bonds are usually not publicized and remain somewhat hidden from society; temporary bonds are often arranged the same way. Depending on the specific situation, the species of the beastfolk individual, and status inside the settlement, this can be desirable, frightening, profitable, humiliating, or prestigious.

Beastfolk sexual culture deeply influenced humans. The settlement is more tolerant of public displays of power over the body, demonstrations of belonging, forceful erotic games, group possession, submissive roles, erotic punishments, keeping a person close to a powerful being, and exchanging people between influential circles. The administration intervenes mainly when there is a threat of death, severe injury, pregnancy without authorization, conflict between powerful families, violation of age boundaries, or risk of exposing the settlement.

Human families respond to this in different ways. Some fear losing a daughter or female relative to a beastfolk individual's orbit. Others see it as strengthening the family's position. Still others spend years building ties with large hybrids, counting on protection and access to closed circles. For some young women, the attention of a massive beastfolk individual becomes a dangerous dream: his power frightens them, but the possibility of being chosen by a being of that level carries a strong social and erotic charge.

Women in the settlement have their own hidden layer of experience: advice, warnings, envy, fear, romanticization of beastfolk, stories about those who were chosen, stories about those who managed to leave, unspoken support, and rivalry for the attention of powerful hybrids. These stories pass through friends, older female relatives, closed conversations, and family warnings.

Young residents who study or temporarily live outside the settlement often begin to understand more sharply how unique their world is. In outside society, beastfolk exist only as myths, folklore, game monsters, ancient gods, cryptids, or fantasy. For settlement residents, they are real beings who can be spoken to, lived beside, feared, desired, hated, respected, or loved.

This experience can strengthen the sense of belonging. Young people realize that they are part of a hidden social system almost no one on the planet knows about. They have access to living history, to beings considered impossible by the rest of the world, and to a culture that has survived two thousand years.

Some young people still choose to leave. Some want an ordinary life, the outside world, a career, personal freedom, or relationships beyond the settlement. In such cases, exit protocols activate: the person's memory is altered, dangerous knowledge is removed, and a safe biography remains so they cannot expose the settlement.

Official services use the language of medicine, security, age, pregnancy, authorization, and risk. In private life, people continue to live by older beastfolk rules. This creates a constant tension between the documents of 2070 and the real culture of the settlement.

Pregnancy from a hybrid carries enormous social weight. It can become prestige, danger, a medical crisis, a cause of family conflict, or a way to secure a young woman's position. Such a child becomes an event for the family, medical services, demographic control, and the settlement's internal politics.

The settlement lives with the internal knowledge that the outside world sees only the thin surface of reality. Beyond the settlement perimeter, dragons, beastfolk, ancient gods, and extraterrestrial civilizations remain myths, theories, folklore, cryptids, religious images, or subjects of mass culture. Inside the settlement, all of this has specific faces, bodies, medical consequences, historical records, and technical protocols.

This knowledge creates a distinct sense of separation from outside humanity among residents. They use the same gadgets, study at the same universities, watch the same films, and read the same scientific articles, but their picture of the world is deeper. They know that part of human history was misunderstood, part of myth grew out of real encounters, and part of the impossible is simply kept securely hidden.

Everyday language inside the settlement is modern and often ironic. Residents nickname the sleeping Herald beneath the city "the big guy downstairs," or, with dry affection, "the landlord," and they call a voluntary, memory-wiped departure "going to zero."

Inside the perimeter, truth is part of the everyday picture of the world. Beastfolk, the Herald, Zero-Point Reality Intersection technology, old events, and the extraterrestrial nature of the technology can be discussed openly if a person has the proper access and a reason to know the details. Secrecy is directed outward. When a resident leaves the settlement, their public biography, documents, and memory enter the external cover regime, where dangerous lines of thought are blocked or redirected toward safe explanations.

Residents develop a culture of double knowledge. There is an external version of the world suitable for universities, documents, conversations with outsiders, and life beyond the field. There is an internal version where ancient images, legends, hybrids, extraterrestrial traces, and the Herald's history form a fuller picture. An educated person from the settlement can live freely in both versions.

The sleeping Herald beneath the settlement gives the city a special depth. Beneath ordinary streets, classrooms, clinics, warehouses, residential quarters, and technical sectors lies a dragon-like extraterrestrial being who has survived human eras and is tied to the reason the settlement exists.

For some residents, the Herald is the center of the entire system around which security, demography, education, and external cover are built. For others, he is a legacy of the distant past, the reason their city still lives in isolation. For others, he is the greatest scientific object on the planet, sleeping beside them under the protection of technologies that outside humanity still cannot fully understand.

This creates a special relationship with time. The settlement thinks in long spans: generations, access levels, inheritance of professions, preservation of memory, population control, specialist training, and the possibility of awakening. In an ordinary city, the future is measured by career, family, and economy. Here this is joined by the question of what will happen to the city if the Herald wakes one day.

Access level to the Herald's systems becomes part of internal status. Field engineers, biosystem specialists, capsule operators, analysts of old records, facility medics, and external cover personnel are perceived as people standing closer to the real center of the settlement. Their status is based on competence, checks, responsibility, and the ability to deal with what the outside world would call impossible.

Outside science looks strong and blind at the same time to settlement residents. It can build AI, treat diseases, launch complex technologies, and analyze ancient cultures, but it passes by the key hidden layer of history. This creates a calm sense of superiority: the outside world is developed and dangerous, but its picture of reality remains incomplete.

Young people feel this contrast especially sharply after studying outside. There, dragons are creature designs, beastfolk are a mythological motif, ancient gods are a cultural symbol, and extraterrestrial life is an open question. At home, all of this returns to reality: to beings they can meet, to the object beneath the settlement, to family stories, and to technologies that support the hidden city every day.

This culture makes residents modern, closed, and internally confident. They live in 2070, use AI, medicine, documents, and outside education, but at the same time they belong to a system that preserves an entire hidden layer of the world: beastfolk, extraterrestrial heritage, distorted human memory, and the sleeping dragon beneath the settlement.

A beastfolk individual in this culture does not have to "reward" a human according to human rules. He takes, holds, protects, punishes, releases, or passes onward by his own will and by the logic of his status. His generosity is possible, but it is perceived as a manifestation of strength, favor, or ownership, not as an equal exchange.

The settlement's central peculiarity is that the outer layer looks modern: medicine, robotics, AI, documents, protocols, services, and education. The inner intimate layer is much more power-driven and beastfolk. There, a large hybrid can be a protector, threat, master, sexual center, symbol of status, and almost superhuman figure for people who live in his shadow.
