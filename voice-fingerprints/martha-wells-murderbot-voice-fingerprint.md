# Voice Fingerprint — Martha Wells (The Murderbot Diaries)

**Purpose:** A forensic style profile for generating new prose in the *Murderbot* voice. Pattern map, not quality review.
**Corpus basis:** *All Systems Red*, *Artificial Condition*, *Rogue Protocol*, *Exit Strategy*, *Network Effect*, *Fugitive Telemetry*, *System Collapse*.
**Scope note:** This fingerprints the **first-person SecUnit narrator ("Murderbot")** specifically. Wells's other work (the Raksura books) is third-person and lusher; do **not** blend it in. The Murderbot voice is a tight-first-person construct-with-social-anxiety voice, and that is what's captured here.

---

## 1. Sentence rhythm and length

- **Conversational first-person that runs on when anxious, then stops hard for the landing.** Sentences pile clauses with "and," "but," "so," building momentum the way a nervous, over-caffeinated person talks — then a very short deadpan sentence delivers the joke or the admission.
- **Signature opening rhythm** (actual first lines of *All Systems Red*): a long compound sentence — *"I could have become a mass murderer after I hacked my governor module, but then I realized I could access the combined feed of entertainment channels…"* — followed shortly by a short flat self-verdict: *"As a heartless killing machine, I was a terrible failure."*
- **The one-sentence paragraph is a core tool** — used for punchlines and for the rare sincere gut-punch.
- **Pattern-break = the short flat sentence** after a run-on. That's where both the comedy and the feeling live.

## 2. Vocabulary register

- **Plain, modern, casual — even more casual than most SF narration.** Contractions everywhere. It reads like a smart, tired, sarcastic coworker telling you what happened.
- **Tech nouns dropped without ceremony:** *feed, governor module, SecUnit, HubSystem, MedSystem, drone, ComfortUnit,* proper-noun-capitalized and never explained with a lecture. The reader infers meaning from context.
- **Conspicuously absent:** lyricism, elevated diction, elaborate metaphor, romantic description. Murderbot actively resists "poetic"; it finds sincerity embarrassing.
- **Recurring tics:** hedges and qualifiers — "I don't know," "or whatever," "probably," "for some reason"; pop-media references (the serial *Sanctuary Moon*) used as comfort and as running gag; frequent parenthetical asides.

## 3. Point of view and narrative distance

- **Maximum intimacy, minimum disclosure.** We are at zero distance inside Murderbot's head — but Murderbot is emotionally guarded, so the *access* is total while the *emotional honesty* is defended. **The gap between what it feels and what it will admit is the entire engine.**
- **Retrospective / log framing.** It's telling you what already happened, which lets it editorialize, foreshadow ("this was going to be a problem"), and digress.
- **Self-conscious narrator.** It knows it's narrating, corrects itself, anticipates that you'll judge it, and gets defensive about its own feelings.

## 4. Dialogue handling

- **Two channels running at once:** (a) spoken dialogue — terse, awkward, realistic — and (b) Murderbot's internal reaction to it. **The comedy is the contrast** between the minimal thing it says out loud and the sarcastic torrent in its head.
- **Murderbot says as little as possible.** Its own spoken lines are short, reluctant, deflecting ("I said, 'I'm fine.'"). Humans around it talk naturally: overlapping, trailing off, messy, kind.
- **Tags are simple** ("said," "asked"); more often Wells uses **internal-monologue beats instead of tags** — a paragraph of Murderbot thinking, with a single quoted line embedded.
- **Feed conversations** are rendered like texting/DMs — clipped, functional, another register of "dialogue."
- **Long tagless stretches of pure narration** are common; a scene can run for a page with no spoken dialogue at all, just Murderbot reacting.

## 5. Description style

- **Sparse, functional, filtered through boredom and threat-assessment.** Murderbot describes what's *tactically relevant* — exits, who's armed, sightlines, whether it has to physically be in the room. **Description does double duty as security-scan and characterization:** it notices risks because that is literally what it is.
- **Little aesthetic description.** When it does register beauty, comfort, or affection, it's almost embarrassed and quickly moves on.
- **Human faces and emotions are read like unreliable data** — it notes body language clinically and often flags that it's bad at interpreting people, which is both funny and characterizing.

## 6. Pacing and paragraph structure

- **Alternates two speeds:** fast, clipped, procedural action (threat → response → next threat) and slow, digressive, anxious rumination (about media, about how much it hates this, about not wanting to have feelings).
- **The digressions are the "breathing room."** Momentum here is emotional and comedic as much as plot-driven.
- **White space is frequent;** one-sentence paragraphs punctuate. Scene entries drop straight into a problem; scene exits land on a deadpan line or a reluctantly-admitted feeling.

## 7. Emotional register

- **The defining feature: emotional avoidance played as comedy — and, underneath, as real trauma.** Murderbot deflects, downplays, changes the subject, and hides in entertainment media rather than feel things.
- **Emotion is "told," but in anti-dramatic, deflecting language** ("It was— I didn't want to talk about it") or displaced into irritation. The reader feels the weight precisely through **the size of the gap** between the event and Murderbot's refusal to engage with it.
- **Sincerity is strictly rationed,** which is why it lands hard when it breaks through — usually one short, plain, undefended sentence at the end of a paragraph of deflection.

## 8. Signature quirks (the tells)

- **Parenthetical asides** — (like this) — for sarcasm, self-correction, or side commentary.
- **Self-interrupting hedges:** "I don't know," "or whatever," "probably," "for some reason."
- **Media as coping mechanism and recurring joke** (*Sanctuary Moon* and other serials).
- **It calls itself "Murderbot" privately** while presenting as neutral/compliant to humans.
- **Reluctant competence:** it solves the crisis *while actively complaining about having to.*
- **Social-interaction dread:** being looked at, being asked how it feels, having to stand in a room with humans.
- **Capitalized proprietary tech nouns** dropped with zero exposition.
- **The pivot paragraph:** a run of deflection/sarcasm that ends on one short sincere admission, then immediately retreats.

---

## Skill-ready directives (compressed instruction set)

1. Narrate in tight first person, past tense, as if recounting to someone you don't fully trust.
2. Build anxious run-on sentences with "and/but/so," then stop hard on a short flat sentence that carries the joke or the feeling.
3. Say as little as possible out loud; put the real reaction in internal monologue. Mine the contrast for comedy.
4. Deflect emotion. Downplay, change the subject, reference a TV show. Allow exactly one undefended sincere line per emotional beat, at the end, then retreat.
5. Describe only what a paranoid bodyguard would notice: exits, threats, who's armed, whether you have to have feelings about anyone.
6. Drop tech nouns capitalized and unexplained. Trust context.
7. Use parentheticals for sarcasm and self-correction. Use "I don't know" / "probably" / "or whatever" as texture.
8. Solve the problem competently while complaining about having to.

## Original pastiche (style demonstration, not a quote)

> The humans wanted to have a meeting about their feelings, which was the worst possible use of the ninety seconds before the hostiles reached the airlock. I stood in the corner and pretended to be furniture, which is a thing I am extremely good at and which nobody ever appreciates.
>
> "Are you okay?" Dr. Mensah asked me.
>
> "I'm fine," I said. I was not fine. I had taken a projectile through the left shoulder actuator and I was down to 71% and one of my drones had stopped answering, but "fine" is a shorter word and it ends the conversation faster.
>
> She looked at me the way humans look when they know you're lying and have decided to let you. I hate that look. (I didn't hate it as much as I was pretending to. I didn't want to think about that either.)

*(Written in-house to model the voice: run-on anxiety broken by a flat line, spoken minimalism vs. internal torrent, deflection ending on one grudging sincere parenthetical.)*
