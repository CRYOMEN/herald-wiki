## Overview

This document catalogs the 25 historical women who became Alekandras' romantic partners during his 3,000-year stay in Ancient Egypt and the Mediterranean world (3200-30 BCE). Created to add depth and historical authenticity to the character's backstory, this reference identifies real historical figures—queens, priestesses, warriors, philosophers, and poets—whose lives and periods align with key moments in Alekandras' timeline throughout ancient history.

### Research Disclaimer

This research was conducted with AI assistance for initial identification, source gathering, and historical cross-referencing. As such, dates, historical interpretations, timeline alignments, and character connections may contain inaccuracies or anachronisms. This document should be considered work-in-progress and requires future scholarly review, fact-checking, and correction by historians specializing in Ancient Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Greek, and Mediterranean studies.

## Detailed Descriptions

### 1. Merneith
**Lifespan:** ~2950 BCE
**Date with Alekandras:** ~2930 BCE
**Period:** Lull, early Old Kingdom

**Circumstances of Meeting:**
The first known female ruler of Egypt, mother of Pharaoh Den. Alekandras, having just begun his journey on Earth, was captivated by her secret power and wisdom. This was his first documented interaction with a mortal ruler.

**Integration into Lore:**
The stable period allowed Alekandras to focus on his first relationships. Merneith initiated a millennia-long tradition—the dragon sought in women not only beauty but strength of spirit capable of ruling a kingdom.

**Connection to Real Events:**
Early 1st Dynasty, regency during her son Den's reign. First mentions of a female ruler in Egyptian chronicles.

**Sources:**

- Ancient Origins: https://www.ancient-origins.net/news-history-archaeology/merneith-tomb-0019466

### 2. Princess Neith
**Lifespan:** ~2900 BCE
**Date with Alekandras:** ~2920 BCE
**Period:** Early Old Kingdom, 2nd Dynasty

**Circumstances of Meeting:**
Daughter of Pharaoh Hotepsekhemwy, priestess of the cult of goddess Neith in Sais. The cult of Neith (patroness of serpents and dragons) attracted Alekandras' attention—he visited her temple in the form of a sacred snake.

**Integration into Lore:**
The connection with the Neith cult became the first "recognition" of Alekandras by local priests. Legends of the "golden serpent" in the temple of Sais may be echoes of these meetings.

**Connection to Real Events:**
2nd Dynasty, cult of Neith in Sais (city in the Nile Delta). Goddess Neith—one of the most ancient Egyptian deities, patroness of hunting and war.

### 3. Priestess of Hathor from Hierakonpolis
**Lifespan:** ~2800 BCE
**Date with Alekandras:** ~2895 BCE
**Period:** Early Old Kingdom, 3rd Dynasty

**Circumstances of Meeting:**
She led a choir of singers and dancers in the main temple of Hathor. Her singing was described as "enchanting to the gods"—Alekandras, a being from another world, was "enchanted" by this power.

**Integration into Lore:**
Art as a distraction from the burden of immortality. For the first time, Alekandras found in mortal art something that touched his heart—beauty as a way to forget the pain of eternity.

**Connection to Real Events:**
3rd Dynasty, cult of Hathor—goddess of love, music, and fertility. Temples of Hathor existed throughout Egypt.

### 4. Puabi
**Lifespan:** ~2600-2500 BCE
**Date with Alekandras:** ~2860 BCE
**Period:** Beginning of the War with Apophis

**Circumstances of Meeting:**
Queen of Ur (Sumer), buried with the richest regalia in tomb PG 800. Alekandras ventured beyond Egypt for the first time—tension before the storm forced him to seek allies even in distant Mesopotamia.

**Integration into Lore:**
Before the war with Apophis began, Alekandras sought allies. The rare artifacts from Puabi's tomb ("gifts from a god")—possibly real traces of his visit to Ur.

**Connection to Real Events:**
Sumer, Ur III period. Puabi's tomb (PG 800)—one of the greatest archaeological discoveries of the 20th century (excavated by Leonard Woolley).

**Sources:**

### 5. Hetepheres I
**Lifespan:** ~2600-2560 BCE
**Date with Alekandras:** ~2790 BCE
**Period:** War with Apophis (Phase 1)

**Circumstances of Meeting:**
Mother of Pharaoh Khufu, whose secret chamber in Giza contained unique treasures. During the war, Alekandras needed allies—the queen as a source of stability.

**Integration into Lore:**
The legend of "eyes seeing through gold"—her secret chamber contained objects of "unknown origin." These were possibly gifts from Alekandras, conveying divine right to her son, the pyramid builder.

**Connection to Real Events:**
4th Dynasty, mother of Khufu (builder of the Great Pyramid). Discovery of her secret chamber in Giza (1925) shocked archaeologists.

### 6. Khentkawes I
**Lifespan:** ~2470 BCE
**Date with Alekandras:** ~2660 BCE
**Period:** Lull after the War

**Circumstances of Meeting:**
Daughter of Menkaure, bore the titles of pharaoh, built her own pyramid in Giza. The recovery period allowed Alekandras deep emotional connections.

**Integration into Lore:**
Her status as "mother of two kings" spawned legends of a "goddess born from a serpent." Alekandras saw in her a bearer of royal blood capable of generating power.

**Connection to Real Events:**
4th-5th Dynasty transitional period. Her unusual tomb (with elements of both pyramid and mastaba) is still studied by Egyptologists.

### 7. Hetaera of Memphis
**Lifespan:** ~2400 BCE
**Date with Alekandras:** ~2440 BCE
**Period:** Lull (Phase 2)

**Circumstances of Meeting:**
Professional performer from the "Khener Corps," performing at the courts of wealthy officials. Stability allowed Alekandras to enjoy art.

**Integration into Lore:**
Legends of the "god who descended to hear a mortal's song." Alekandras attended her performance disguised as a noble foreigner, seeking in art to forget the burden of immortality.

**Connection to Real Events:**
5th Dynasty, the "Khener Corps"—professional musicians and dancers depicted in tomb paintings.

### 8. Enheduanna
**Lifespan:** ~2285-2250 BCE
**Date with Alekandras:** ~2220 BCE
**Period:** Transitional Period

**Circumstances of Meeting:**
The first known poetess in history, daughter of Sargon of Akkad, high priestess of Nanna. An intellectual challenge in crisis times—Alekandras sought an equal in the art of words.

**Integration into Lore:**
Her poetry about divine love may have been inspired by meeting a being possessing divine wisdom. The "golden dragon" of Sumerian mythology found his equal.

**Connection to Real Events:**
Sumer/Akkad, daughter of Sargon of Akkad (founder of the Akkadian Empire). Author of the "Hymns of Enheduanna"—the first literary texts with a known author.

### 9. Sobekneferu
**Lifespan:** ~1806-1802 BCE
**Date with Alekandras:** ~2000 BCE
**Period:** Middle Kingdom, 12th Dynasty

**Circumstances of Meeting:**
The first confirmed female pharaoh, took the name of the crocodile god Sobek. Power over the waters of the Nile as an alliance in the war against chaos.

**Integration into Lore:**
Sobek's name (sacred crocodile/dragon) is associated with forces of chaos. Alekandras saw in her a kindred spirit in a worshipper of serpent deity, may have been her "secret advisor."

**Connection to Real Events:**
12th Dynasty, last ruler of the dynasty. The name Sobek-neferu ("Sobek is beautiful") indicates the cult of the crocodile god.

### 10. Snake Goddess Priestess (Knossos)
**Lifespan:** ~1600-1450 BCE
**Date with Alekandras:** ~1780 BCE
**Period:** Middle Kingdom, Rise

**Circumstances of Meeting:**
Priestess in Crete who wielded sacred snakes and predicted earthquakes. The connection of snakes to dragons drew Alekandras—he appeared as the "father of serpents."

**Integration into Lore:**
Expansion of influence beyond Egypt. The meeting in the Cretan labyrinth may have become the myth of Theseus and the Minotaur in distorted transmission.

**Connection to Real Events:**
Minoan civilization, Knossos. The famous Snake Goddess figurines from Knossos (kept in the Heraklion Museum).

### 20. Arete of Cyrene
**Lifespan:** ~400-340 BCE
**Date with Alekandras:** ~50 BCE
**Period:** Late Ptolemaic

**Circumstances of Meeting:**
Daughter of Aristippus, leader of the Cyrenaic school of philosophy. Philosophy of pleasure before departure.

**Integration into Lore:**
Like Hypatia, Arete was a female intellectual who led a philosophical school in a man's world. Cyrenaica (Libya) was within Egypt's sphere of influence, and her philosophy of life's pleasures interested Alekandras—a being who survived millennia of observations sought meaning in the simple joys of mortals.

**Connection to Real Events:**
Ancient Greece, Cyrene (Libya), 4th century BCE. Daughter of Aristippus, founder of the Cyrenaic school, preaching hedonistic ethics.

### 25. Cleopatra VII
**Lifespan:** 69-30 BCE
**Date with Alekandras:** ~30-25 BCE
**Period:** Before Hibernation

**Circumstances of Meeting:**
Last pharaoh of Ancient Egypt, ruling during the critical period of independence's fall before Rome. Friend who became lover—symbol of an era's end.

**Integration into Lore:**
Her connection to goddess Isis and position as divine pharaoh make her a natural object of attention for the dragon-guardian of Amun-Ra. Their meeting became a symbol of passing eras: from ancient Egypt to Roman rule, from gods to emperors. Cleopatra gifted Iras to Alekandras, and then their relationship evolved into romance in the final years before Egypt's fall.

**Connection to Real Events:**
Ptolemaic Kingdom, reign 51-30 BCE. Last pharaoh of Ancient Egypt, connections with Caesar and Mark Antony, suicide in 30 BCE.

## Connection to the War Against Apophis

### War Timeline

| Phase | Dates | Events | Women of This Period |
|------|------|---------|----------------------|
| Preparation | ~2900-2700 BCE | Alekandras senses approaching threat, seeks allies | Puabi (Sumer) |
| Phase 1 | ~2700-2600 BCE | War begins, biosuit energy depletion | Hetepheres I |
| Lull 1 | ~2600-2400 BCE | Recovery, pyramid construction | Merneith, Princess Neith, Priestess of Hathor, Khentkawes I, Hetaera of Memphis |
| Phase 2 | ~2400-2300 BCE | Second wave, creation of monster army | Enheduanna |
| Lull 2 | ~2300-2100 BCE | Prolonged peace | Sobekneferu, Snake Goddess Priestess |
| Hyksos | ~1650-1550 BCE | Invasion, resistance | Ahhotep I |

### Key Connections

**Puabi (~2860 BCE)** — First foray beyond Egypt seeking allies before war. Sumer as potential source of knowledge about ancient threats.

**Hetepheres I (~2790 BCE)** — Alliance with 4th Dynasty royal family to mobilize resources. "Strange golden weapons" in her trophies—Alekandras' technologies.

**Enheduanna (~2220 BCE)** — Intellectual alliance during second phase of war. Sumerian wisdom as key to understanding Apophis' nature.

**Ahhotep I (~1560 BCE)** — Military alliance against Hyksos as echo of war with Apophis. "Golden weapons" reappear in Egyptian queen's arsenal.
